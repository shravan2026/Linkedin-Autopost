Medical writing vs medical communications — let's settle this debate with actual 2026 salary data.

I've been in medical writing for 15+ years. I've mentored professionals in both tracks. And the most common question I get is: "Which one pays more?"

Here's the honest breakdown:

MEDICAL WRITING (Regulatory):
→ Entry-level (India): ₹6-10 LPA
→ Mid-level (5-8 years): ₹15-25 LPA
→ Senior/Lead (10+ years): ₹30-50 LPA
→ US-based roles: $95K-$160K
→ Freelance (experienced): $80-$150/hour

MEDICAL COMMUNICATIONS:
→ Entry-level (India): ₹5-8 LPA
→ Mid-level (5-8 years): ₹12-20 LPA
→ Senior/Lead (10+ years): ₹25-40 LPA
→ US-based roles: $85K-$140K
→ Freelance (experienced): $70-$120/hour

The verdict? Regulatory medical writing pays more at senior levels — consistently.

But here's the nuance:

1. MedComms offers faster career progression. You can become an Account Director or VP at an agency by year 10.
2. Regulatory writing has higher barriers to entry (science degree + therapeutic expertise required).
3. MedComms is more creative — publications, slide decks, KOL engagement.
4. Regulatory writing offers more job security — submissions don't stop during market downturns.
5. MedComms professionals can pivot into commercial roles more easily.

My take after 15 years:

If you want stability, deep science, and top-tier compensation → regulatory medical writing.
If you want variety, speed, and agency-style growth → medical communications.

Both are excellent careers. But know what you're optimizing for.
