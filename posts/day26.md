Remote Work in Medical Writing – The New Normal

Medical writing was one of the first functions in pharma to prove that remote work doesn't just work — it thrives.

Having worked remotely for several years now, here's my honest assessment of what's changed:

✅ What works well:
- Deep focus work — writing complex documents requires uninterrupted concentration. Home offices deliver this.
- Global collaboration — I work with teams across time zones daily. Remote infrastructure makes this seamless.
- Work-life integration — no commute means more time for professional development, family, and health.
- Access to talent — companies now hire the best writers regardless of geography.

⚠️ What requires intention:
- Spontaneous collaboration — you have to deliberately create moments for informal knowledge sharing.
- Mentoring junior writers — screen-sharing a document review isn't quite the same as sitting beside someone.
- Meeting fatigue — without physical cues, virtual meetings can drain energy faster.
- Boundaries — when your office is your home, "logging off" requires discipline.

My approach:

I maintain structured daily routines, block focus time for writing, and protect boundaries between work hours and personal time. I also make it a point to have regular 1:1 conversations with team members beyond project discussions.

Remote work in medical writing is here to stay. The writers who thrive are those who treat it as a skill to develop, not just a perk to enjoy.
