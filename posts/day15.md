Regulatory Writing for FDA vs EMA — Key Differences Every Medical Writer Should Know

If you've written for both FDA and EMA submissions, you know they're not interchangeable. While ICH guidelines provide a common foundation, the execution differs significantly.

Here's what I've observed across 15+ years of regulatory writing:

📋 **Document Structure:**
- FDA favors the CTD format with specific guidance on Module 2.5 (Clinical Overview) and 2.7 (Clinical Summary)
- EMA requires additional documents like the RMP (mandatory) and may request a Pediatric Investigation Plan

📋 **Writing Tone:**
- FDA submissions tend to be data-dense and direct
- EMA often expects more contextual discussion around benefit-risk and public health impact

📋 **Safety Reporting:**
- FDA relies heavily on the ISS (Integrated Summary of Safety)
- EMA emphasizes the PSUR/PBRER framework for post-marketing safety

📋 **Labeling:**
- USPI follows a structured format with Highlights section
- SmPC has its own template with a different organizational logic

📋 **Queries & Interactions:**
- FDA issues Complete Response Letters and Information Requests
- EMA works through Day 120/180 List of Questions and oral explanations

**The takeaway:** A medical writer working on global programs must be bilingual in regulatory expectations. What satisfies one agency may generate questions from another.

Understanding these nuances is what separates a competent writer from a strategic partner in regulatory submissions.
