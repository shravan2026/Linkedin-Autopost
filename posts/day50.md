Indian pharma companies are on a buying spree — acquiring US and EU companies at a pace we've never seen before.

And it's not just about generics anymore. This is a strategic pivot toward innovation.

Look at the data (2024-2026):

→ Mankind Pharma acquired BSV Group for ₹13,630 crore — largest domestic pharma deal
→ Torrent Pharma acquired Curatio Healthcare for ₹2,000 crore
→ Lupin acquired licensed rights from Boehringer Ingelheim for key respiratory assets
→ Aurobindo Pharma expanded injectables portfolio through US acquisitions
→ Sun Pharma invested heavily in specialty and innovation pipelines globally
→ Dr. Reddy's acquired select brands from Mayne Pharma in the US market
→ Intas (now Accord Healthcare) operates as a top-10 global generics company

Total Indian pharma M&A value in 2024-2025: Estimated $8-10 billion+

Why this acceleration?

1. Patent cliffs: Major biologics losing exclusivity (Humira, Keytruda by 2028) create massive opportunity.
2. US market access: Acquiring established US/EU companies gives instant distribution and regulatory infrastructure.
3. Biosimilar ambitions: Building capabilities requires acquiring technology, not just licensing it.
4. Valuation arbitrage: Indian companies trading at premium multiples are using strong balance sheets to acquire undervalued Western assets.

What this means for Indian pharma professionals:

→ More global roles being created out of India
→ Regulatory writing for US/EU markets becoming a core India-based function
→ Quality, pharmacovigilance, and medical affairs teams expanding rapidly

India isn't just manufacturing for the world anymore. We're buying our way into innovation — and backing it with execution.
