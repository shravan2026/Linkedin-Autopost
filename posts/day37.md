3 clinical trials in 2025 didn't just change medicine — they rewrote what we thought was possible.

And having worked in medical writing for 15+ years, I can tell you: the documentation behind these trials was as exceptional as the science.

Here are the 3 trials that will be studied for decades:

1. DESTINY-Breast06 (Daiichi Sankyo/AstraZeneca)
Trastuzumab deruxtecan in HER2-ultralow breast cancer.
→ Expanded treatment to patients previously considered "HER2 negative"
→ Redefined an entire cancer classification
→ The CSR had to establish a NEW biomarker threshold — documentation that literally created a treatment category

2. TRIDENT-1 Long-term Follow-up (Takeda)
Repotrectinib in ROS1+ NSCLC.
→ 5-year survival data showing durable responses
→ Set new standard for how long-term extension study reports should be structured
→ Integrated real-world evidence alongside clinical data in a single submission

3. CagriSema Phase 3 (Novo Nordisk)
Cagrilintide + Semaglutide combination for obesity.
→ 25%+ body weight reduction
→ Patient-reported outcomes documentation was groundbreaking
→ First major trial to include mental health endpoints alongside metabolic ones

What made their documentation exceptional:

✓ Crystal-clear primary endpoint definitions
✓ Proactive protocol amendments with transparent rationale
✓ Integrated benefit-risk narratives (not just "here are the results")
✓ Plain language summaries that patients could actually understand

The lesson? Brilliant science fails without brilliant documentation. Regulatory success isn't just about data — it's about telling the story compellingly and precisely.
