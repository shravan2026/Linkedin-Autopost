Common Mistakes in Clinical Study Reports (And How to Avoid Them)

The CSR is the cornerstone of clinical trial documentation. Yet after reviewing and authoring hundreds of them, I still see the same mistakes repeated. Here are the most common — and how to fix them:

❌ **Inconsistency between sections** — The methods section says one thing, the results section implies another. Always cross-reference your protocol, SAP, and TLFs when writing.

❌ **Burying key findings** — Regulators don't have time to hunt. Lead with the primary endpoint results. Be explicit about whether objectives were met.

❌ **Ignoring the narrative flow** — A CSR isn't just a data dump. It should tell a coherent story: why the study was done, what happened, and what it means.

❌ **Inconsistent terminology** — Using "subjects" in one section and "participants" in another. Pick your terms and stick with them throughout.

❌ **Poorly written safety sections** — Adverse event summaries need context. Don't just list — interpret. Was an SAE related to treatment? What was the outcome?

❌ **Not following ICH E3 structure** — It exists for a reason. Deviating without justification creates unnecessary regulatory queries.

**My advice:** Before finalizing any CSR, do a "cold read" — read it as if you've never seen the study before. If something confuses you, it will confuse the reviewer.

Quality CSRs don't just document a trial. They facilitate regulatory decisions.
