Safety Narratives — Telling the Patient's Story with Scientific Precision

Of all the documents I write, safety narratives hold a special place. Each one represents a real person who experienced a serious adverse event during a clinical trial.

The challenge? Balancing human empathy with scientific objectivity.

Here's what makes a strong safety narrative:

🔹 **Chronological clarity** — Present events in the order they occurred. The reviewer should be able to follow the timeline without confusion: onset, interventions, outcomes.

🔹 **Clinical context** — Don't write in a vacuum. Include relevant medical history, concomitant medications, and baseline conditions that may explain or contextualize the event.

🔹 **Causality assessment** — Clearly state the investigator's and sponsor's assessment of relatedness. If opinions differ, present both transparently.

🔹 **Outcome precision** — Resolved, resolving, not resolved, fatal, or unknown. Be specific about the outcome at the time of reporting and at study completion.

🔹 **Conciseness without loss** — Every word should serve a purpose. Avoid editorializing, but ensure no clinically relevant detail is omitted.

🔹 **Regulatory compliance** — Follow CIOMS format requirements and company-specific templates consistently.

**What drives me:** Behind every narrative is a patient who trusted the clinical trial process. Our job is to ensure their experience is documented accurately so that safety decisions are informed and future patients are protected.

That's not just writing. That's responsibility.
