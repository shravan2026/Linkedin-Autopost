A single dose of Casgevy costs $2.2 million. And it CURES sickle cell disease.

Welcome to the era of Cell and Gene Therapy — where medicine isn't treating disease anymore. It's rewriting human biology.

The numbers are mind-blowing:

→ 3,700+ cell and gene therapy clinical trials active globally (2025)
→ 15 CGT products approved by the FDA to date
→ Market projected to reach $97 billion by 2030
→ Average development timeline: 10-15 years and $1-4 billion per therapy

The approved therapies are revolutionary:
• Casgevy (Vertex/CRISPR) — First CRISPR-based gene editing therapy
• Zolgensma (Novartis) — Single-dose cure for spinal muscular atrophy ($2.1M)
• Kymriah (Novartis) — CAR-T therapy for blood cancers
• Luxturna (Spark) — Gene therapy restoring vision in inherited blindness

But here's what most people don't realize about the documentation challenge:

Traditional clinical trials have predictable endpoints and timelines. CGT trials are fundamentally different:
→ Tiny patient populations (sometimes N=20-50)
→ No established surrogate endpoints
→ Long-term follow-up mandated (15 years for some gene therapies)
→ Manufacturing IS the product (autologous therapies are patient-specific)
→ Regulatory frameworks still evolving globally

For medical writers, CGT documents require entirely new competencies — genomics literacy, manufacturing process understanding, adaptive trial design expertise, and real-world evidence integration.

India is entering this space too. Immuneel Therapeutics (CAR-T), Stempeutics (stem cells), and IIT Bombay's gene therapy programs are pioneering affordable CGT for Indian patients.

The biggest bottleneck isn't science. It's infrastructure, documentation standards, and regulatory alignment.
