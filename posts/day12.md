Working with Cross-Functional Teams in Clinical Development

Medical writing doesn't happen in isolation. Every document we produce is the result of collaboration with biostatisticians, clinical scientists, regulatory strategists, pharmacovigilance teams, and medical monitors.

After 15+ years navigating these collaborations, here's what I've learned about making cross-functional teamwork effective:

🔹 **Speak their language** — Statisticians think in SAPs and TLFs. Clinicians think in patient outcomes. Regulatory teams think in submission timelines. Adapt your communication style to your audience.

🔹 **Set expectations early** — Define review timelines, comment resolution processes, and escalation paths before the first draft goes out. Ambiguity breeds delay.

🔹 **Be the connector** — Medical writers often have the broadest view of a study's documentation landscape. Use that vantage point to flag inconsistencies across documents that siloed teams might miss.

🔹 **Manage conflicting feedback gracefully** — When a clinician's comment contradicts a statistician's input, don't just pick one. Facilitate a discussion. The resolution often leads to a stronger document.

🔹 **Respect expertise** — You don't need to be the subject matter expert on everything. Know when to ask questions and when to defer to the specialist in the room.

The best clinical documents I've worked on at Bristol Myers Squibb and Syneos Health were always the ones where the team communicated openly, respected timelines, and shared a common goal: getting the science right.
