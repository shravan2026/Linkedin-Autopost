15 Years in Medical Writing – Key Lessons and What's Next

Fifteen years. Hundreds of documents. Multiple therapeutic areas. Two very different work environments. Here's what I know now that I wish I'd known at the start:

1️⃣ Writing is only 40% of the job – The rest is project management, stakeholder alignment, and strategic thinking.

2️⃣ Say no to ambiguity – If the data isn't clear, ask questions before writing. A vague CSR helps no one.

3️⃣ Build relationships across functions – The best documents come from writers who collaborate deeply with statisticians, clinicians, and regulatory teams.

4️⃣ Specialization and breadth both have value – I've done CSRs, protocols, IBs, ICFs, RMPs, and clinical summaries. Breadth made me versatile; depth made me valuable.

5️⃣ Your first draft is never your best draft – Revision is where quality lives. Protect time for it.

6️⃣ Regulatory intelligence is a superpower – Understanding why an agency asks for something helps you anticipate what they need.

7️⃣ Mentor others – Teaching junior writers sharpens your own skills and builds a stronger profession.

What's next?

The integration of AI, real-world evidence, and decentralized trial designs will reshape our documents. Medical writers who adapt — who become data storytellers and regulatory strategists — will lead the next chapter.

I'm grateful for every challenge these 15 years have brought. The best is still ahead.

Thank you to everyone who's been part of this journey.
