India committed ₹15,000 crore ($1.8 billion) to pharma through the PLI scheme. Three years in, here's what's actually happening on the ground.

The Production-Linked Incentive scheme for pharmaceuticals was designed to reduce India's dependence on Chinese APIs and boost domestic manufacturing of complex drugs. The results are... mixed but promising.

What's working:

→ 55 companies selected across 3 categories (KSMs/APIs, complex generics, repurposed drugs)
→ ₹3,200 crore in incremental sales reported in Year 2
→ API imports from China reduced from 68% to 58% for targeted molecules
→ New manufacturing capacity: 35+ greenfield projects announced
→ Fermentation-based API production (the big gap) finally getting investment

The companies making moves:
• Laurus Labs — Built dedicated API facilities for key starting materials
• Aurobindo — ₹3,000 crore investment in penicillin and cephalosporin intermediates
• Divis Laboratories — Expanding backward integration significantly
• Hikal — Complex API manufacturing for regulated markets

What's NOT working yet:
→ Utilization rates at new facilities still below 60%
→ Smaller companies struggling to meet eligibility thresholds
→ Complex generics (Category 2) seeing slower adoption
→ Environmental clearances delaying several projects

The bigger picture: India still imports ~$4 billion in APIs annually, primarily from China. Key dependencies remain for antibiotics (Amoxicillin, Azithromycin), anti-diabetics (Metformin), and cardiovascular drugs.

From a documentation perspective, these new manufacturing facilities generate massive regulatory workloads — site master files, ASMF updates, variation applications across 100+ markets simultaneously.

The PLI scheme isn't a magic bullet. But it's the first serious attempt at pharmaceutical self-reliance India has made. The next 3 years will determine if it's a policy success or an expensive experiment.
