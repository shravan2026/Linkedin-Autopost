## How AI is Transforming Medical Writing (But Not Replacing Writers)

Artificial intelligence is reshaping nearly every knowledge profession, and medical writing is no exception. Having recently completed certification in AI/ML Prompt Engineering, I have been thinking deeply about where this technology fits — and where it does not.

What AI can do well in our field:
- Accelerate first drafts by synthesizing structured data into narrative frameworks
- Assist with literature searches and reference management
- Flag inconsistencies across large document sets
- Generate templates and standardize formatting

What AI cannot do:
- Apply regulatory judgment about what to include, emphasize, or contextualize
- Interpret ambiguous safety signals in the context of a drug's benefit-risk profile
- Navigate the nuanced communication between sponsor, regulator, and investigator
- Take accountability for the accuracy and integrity of a submission document

The medical writer's value has never been in the act of typing. It is in the scientific judgment, regulatory knowledge, and strategic thinking that determines what gets written and how.

AI is a powerful tool in our workflow — much like statistical software is for biostatisticians. It augments capability without replacing the professional who wields it.

My advice to fellow medical writers: engage with these tools. Learn prompt engineering. Understand what large language models can and cannot do. The writers who thrive in the next decade will be those who integrate AI into their practice while maintaining the rigor that regulators demand.
