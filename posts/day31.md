India supplies 20% of the world's generic medicines. But spends less than 0.7% of pharma revenue on R&D.

Let that satisfying contradiction sink in.

We proudly call ourselves the "Pharmacy of the World" — and rightfully so. Indian pharma exports crossed $27.9 billion in FY2025. We supply 40% of generic demand in the US. 60% of global vaccines come from India.

But here's what nobody talks about at industry conferences:

India's pharma R&D spend? Roughly $2.5 billion annually.
Pfizer alone spent $10.7 billion in 2024.
Johnson & Johnson? $15.1 billion.

We're a manufacturing powerhouse running on someone else's innovation engine.

The numbers tell a brutal story:
→ Of the top 100 drugs globally, India originated exactly ZERO novel molecules in the last decade
→ Sun Pharma, our largest company, spends ~7% of revenue on R&D. Roche spends 27%.
→ India has 3,000+ pharma companies. Fewer than 20 have serious NCE (New Chemical Entity) programs.

Why does this matter?

Because generics face a margin squeeze. API prices from China are rising. Regulatory costs are increasing. The easy money era of "copy and manufacture" is slowly ending.

Companies like Biocon, Zydus, and Sun Pharma are making moves — but the gap between where we are and where we need to be is staggering.

The next decade will separate Indian pharma companies that invested in innovation from those that didn't.

My take after 15+ years in this industry: India doesn't have a manufacturing problem. It has an ambition problem in research.

Do you think India can realistically become a drug innovation hub by 2035, or will we always be the world's contract manufacturer?
