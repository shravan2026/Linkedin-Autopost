## Career Transition in Pharma — From QA to Medical Writing

One of the questions I receive most frequently: "How do I transition into medical writing from another pharma role?" Having navigated my own career evolution and guided others through theirs, here is my perspective.

Medical writing attracts professionals from diverse backgrounds — clinical research associates, pharmacovigilance specialists, quality assurance professionals, regulatory affairs coordinators, and academic researchers. Each brings unique strengths to the table.

If you are in QA or a related function and considering this transition:

**Leverage what you already know.** QA professionals understand documentation standards, SOPs, regulatory expectations, and audit readiness. These are directly transferable. You already think in terms of compliance, traceability, and precision — qualities every medical writer needs.

**Fill the gaps intentionally.** Invest in understanding ICH guidelines (particularly E3, E6, E8, E9), clinical trial design, and biostatistics fundamentals. Take courses in medical writing specifically — AMWA and DIA offer excellent programs.

**Start writing.** Volunteer for document review opportunities. Offer to draft sections of study documents. Build a portfolio that demonstrates your ability to synthesize scientific data into clear narratives.

**Network within your organization.** Medical writing teams often appreciate cross-functional collaboration. Express your interest, seek informational conversations, and ask about shadowing opportunities.

The transition is achievable. It requires deliberate effort, patience, and a willingness to start at a level that may feel below your experience. But the career growth and intellectual satisfaction on the other side make it worthwhile.
