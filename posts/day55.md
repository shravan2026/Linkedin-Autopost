India's biotech sector hit $130 billion in valuation — but we have a massive medical writing talent shortage that nobody is addressing.

The numbers tell a paradoxical story:

INDIA'S BIOTECH GROWTH:
→ 5,000+ biotech startups as of 2025
→ Sector growing at 14-16% CAGR
→ Biocon, Serum Institute, Bharat Biotech — global household names
→ 700+ biosimilar products in various stages of development
→ India accounts for ~30% of global ANDA filings

THE TALENT GAP:
→ Estimated demand for medical writers in India: 25,000-30,000
→ Current workforce: ~12,000-15,000
→ Annual gap: Growing by 2,000-3,000 positions unfilled
→ Only 5-6 dedicated medical writing courses exist in Indian universities
→ Most writers enter the field "accidentally" from pharma, life sciences, or clinical research backgrounds

Why this gap exists:

1. Awareness problem: Most science graduates don't even know medical writing is a career.
2. Training infrastructure: No standardized curriculum. No national certification.
3. Perception issue: Writing is seen as "lesser" than bench research or clinical practice.
4. Industry neglect: Companies hire writers but rarely invest in structured training programs.

The opportunity is ENORMOUS:

→ A trained medical writer can earn ₹15-20 LPA within 5 years
→ Remote work options with global companies
→ Freelancers earning ₹1-2 lakh/month are increasingly common
→ Therapeutic specialization (oncology, immunology) commands premium rates

If you're a pharmacist, life science graduate, or MBBS doctor looking for a career pivot — medical writing in India's biotech boom is your window.

The industry is growing. The talent isn't keeping up. That's your opportunity.
