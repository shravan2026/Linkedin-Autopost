## Mentoring Junior Medical Writers — Lessons from 15 Years

One of the most fulfilling aspects of a long career in medical writing is the opportunity to mentor the next generation. Over 15 years, I have worked with dozens of junior writers, and certain lessons come up repeatedly.

Here is what I share with every new medical writer:

**Understand the "why" before the "how."** Do not just follow a template mechanically. Understand why each section exists, what the reviewer needs from it, and how it fits into the broader regulatory narrative.

**Read more documents than you write.** Early in your career, study approved CSRs, published protocols, and successful submission documents. Pattern recognition is your fastest teacher.

**Ask questions without hesitation.** No one expects a junior writer to know everything. What distinguishes strong juniors is their curiosity and willingness to clarify ambiguity early — not after three rounds of review.

**Master the data before you write.** A medical writer who does not understand the statistical outputs or safety tables will produce superficial narratives. Go deep into the data.

**Develop therapeutic area expertise.** Generalists are valuable, but specialists are indispensable. Find an area that excites you and build depth.

**Embrace feedback.** Every red-lined comment from a reviewer is a learning opportunity. The best writers I know still welcome critique after decades in the field.

The industry needs skilled medical writers. If you are early in your journey, invest in your craft. The ceiling in this profession is high.
