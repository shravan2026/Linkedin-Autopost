70 million Indians live with rare diseases. The number of clinical trials conducted for rare diseases in India? Less than 50 in the last decade.

This isn't just a healthcare gap. It's a moral failure of our system.

The numbers are devastating:
→ 7,000+ known rare diseases globally
→ India has treatment available for fewer than 5% of them
→ 95% of rare diseases have NO approved treatment anywhere in the world
→ Average diagnostic journey in India: 7-8 years (vs 4-5 years in the US)
→ Out-of-pocket expenditure for rare disease patients: ₹5-20 lakhs annually

Why aren't clinical trials happening in India for rare diseases?

1. Patient identification — No national rare disease registry until recently (NRDP 2021 only covers 20 diseases). You can't study patients you can't find.

2. Economic incentives — Orphan drug legislation in India is weak. The US Orphan Drug Act offers 7 years market exclusivity + tax credits. India offers... almost nothing.

3. Infrastructure — Most rare disease expertise sits in 5-6 centers of excellence. Decentralized trial infrastructure barely exists.

4. Regulatory gaps — CDSCO has no formal expedited pathway specifically for rare disease drugs (unlike FDA's Accelerated Approval or EMA's PRIME).

5. Commercial viability — With per-patient treatment costs of ₹10-80 lakhs/year and no insurance coverage, sponsors don't see ROI.

What needs to change:
✓ Robust orphan drug policy with real incentives
✓ National patient registry expansion beyond 20 diseases
✓ Regulatory pathways allowing extrapolation from global data
✓ Public-private partnerships for clinical trials
✓ Newborn screening programs identifying patients early

Some progress exists — AIIMS, CMC Vellore, and IGICH Bangalore are building rare disease programs. Sanofi and Takeda have compassionate access programs.

But 70 million patients deserve more than compassion. They deserve clinical evidence and approved therapies.
