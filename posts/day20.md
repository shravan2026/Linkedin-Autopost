Building a Career in Medical Writing — Advice for Fresh Pharmacists

I frequently get messages from B.Pharm and M.Pharm graduates asking: "How do I get into medical writing?"

Having built my career over 15+ years — from early roles to Scientific Writer II at Bristol Myers Squibb — here's my honest advice:

🔹 **Your pharmacy background is your foundation, not your ceiling** — You already understand drug development, pharmacology, and clinical research. That's a head start most other candidates don't have.

🔹 **Learn the regulatory landscape** — Understand ICH guidelines (especially E3, E6, M4), CTD structure, and how documents fit into the submission lifecycle. This separates serious candidates from casual ones.

🔹 **Start with what's accessible** — Clinical research organizations (CROs) often hire freshers for entry-level medical writing roles. Don't dismiss these — they offer incredible exposure to diverse therapeutic areas and document types.

🔹 **Build your writing samples** — Write mock CSR synopses, protocol summaries, or literature reviews. Show potential employers you can write with clarity and scientific rigor.

🔹 **Invest in certifications** — AMWA, EMWA, or DIA courses signal commitment to the profession.

🔹 **Network intentionally** — Connect with medical writers on LinkedIn. Ask thoughtful questions. Attend webinars. Visibility matters.

🔹 **Be patient but persistent** — The first role is the hardest to get. Once you're in, your growth trajectory accelerates rapidly.

This field rewards curiosity, precision, and continuous learning. If that describes you — medical writing might be your calling.
