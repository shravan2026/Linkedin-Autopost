The Pfizer-Seagen deal ($43 billion) fundamentally changed the oncology landscape — and the ripple effects are still unfolding.

This wasn't just a mega-merger. It was Pfizer betting its future on antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

Here's why this deal matters for everyone in drug development:

The numbers:
→ Deal value: $43 billion (closed December 2023)
→ Seagen's ADC portfolio: Adcetris, Padcev, Tukysa, Tivdak
→ Padcev (enfortumab vedotin) alone projected to exceed $8 billion peak sales
→ Pfizer's oncology revenue target by 2030: $20+ billion (from ~$12B today)

What ADCs represent:
→ The "guided missile" approach to cancer treatment
→ Combining monoclonal antibody precision with cytotoxic payload power
→ 100+ ADCs currently in clinical development globally
→ Companies racing: Daiichi Sankyo, AstraZeneca, Gilead, AbbVie, GSK

For medical writers, this is significant:

1. ADC documentation is uniquely complex — you're writing about a biologic AND a small molecule simultaneously.
2. Manufacturing sections are dense: conjugation chemistry, drug-antibody ratio, linker stability.
3. Safety narratives for ADCs are nuanced: target-mediated toxicity vs payload-related toxicity vs off-target effects.
4. Combination trial designs (ADC + checkpoint inhibitor) create exponentially complex CSRs.

The Pfizer-Seagen deal signaled to the industry: ADCs are the next blockbuster category after checkpoint inhibitors.

If you're a medical writer specializing in oncology — ADC expertise will be your golden ticket for the next decade.
