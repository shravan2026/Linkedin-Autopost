5 regulatory trends that will completely reshape pharma submissions by 2028 — and most companies aren't ready.

After 15 years of writing regulatory documents, I've learned to spot seismic shifts early. Here are 5 that are already underway:

1. AI-ASSISTED REGULATORY WRITING
→ FDA is developing frameworks for AI-generated content in submissions
→ EMA's Big Data Steering Group is exploring AI validation standards
→ By 2028: expect AI-drafted first versions of CSRs, protocols, and IBs with human oversight
→ Writers won't be replaced — but writers who use AI will replace those who don't

2. REAL-WORLD EVIDENCE (RWE) IN SUBMISSIONS
→ FDA accepted 100+ submissions with RWE components in 2023-2024
→ RWD sources: EHRs, claims data, registries, wearables
→ New writing challenge: integrating RWE narratives with traditional trial data

3. ROLLING SUBMISSIONS BECOMING STANDARD
→ Accelerated pathways (Breakthrough, PRIME, SAKIGAKE) now expect modular submissions
→ Writers must produce "submission-ready" modules in parallel with ongoing trials

4. PATIENT-FOCUSED DOCUMENTATION
→ FDA's Patient-Focused Drug Development (PFDD) mandates patient experience data
→ Lay summaries of clinical trials now required in EU (CTR 536/2014)
→ Writing for patients AND regulators simultaneously is a new skill

5. GLOBAL HARMONIZATION (FINALLY) ACCELERATING
→ ICH M4 revision modernizing CTD structure
→ ASEAN, African harmonization initiatives reducing redundant submissions
→ One global dossier strategy becoming feasible for more products

Bottom line: The medical writer of 2028 will need skills in AI tools, data science literacy, patient communication, AND traditional regulatory expertise.
