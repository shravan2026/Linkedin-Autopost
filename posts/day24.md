Data Visualization in Clinical Documents – Beyond Tables and Listings

Tables and listings have been the backbone of clinical documents for decades. But in 2026, are we leveraging data visualization to its full potential?

Regulatory agencies are increasingly receptive to well-designed figures that tell a story more effectively than rows of numbers. Here's where I see opportunity:

📊 Kaplan-Meier curves – Already standard in oncology CSRs, but their placement and annotation can be optimized for clarity.

📊 Forest plots – For subgroup analyses, a well-constructed forest plot communicates treatment effects across populations instantly.

📊 Swimmer plots – These elegantly show individual patient timelines, response durations, and treatment exposure in a single figure.

📊 Exposure-response relationships – Visual displays of PK/PD data help regulators understand dose-response better than dense tabular data.

📊 Safety dashboards – For periodic safety reports, visual heat maps of adverse events by system organ class and severity tell the safety story at a glance.

What medical writers should know:

- Figures must be interpretable in black and white (regulatory requirement)
- Every figure needs a clear, self-explanatory title and footnotes
- Work with your biostatistician early to design visuals that serve the narrative
- Less is more — don't visualize everything, visualize what matters

The future of clinical documents is visual. Medical writers who understand data visualization will lead the next evolution of our field.
