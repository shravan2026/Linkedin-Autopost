Decentralized clinical trials aren't the future — they're already here. And medical writers who don't adapt will be left behind.

The pandemic didn't just accelerate DCTs. It permanently rewired how clinical research operates.

The numbers are staggering:

→ DCT market size: $9.3 billion in 2023, projected $23.8 billion by 2028 (CAGR 20.7%)
→ 75% of pharma companies now incorporate at least one decentralized element in trials
→ Patient retention improves by 30-40% with hybrid/decentralized models
→ Companies leading the charge: Medable, Science 37, ICON, Parexel

So what does this mean for medical writers?

Everything changes:

1. Informed Consent Forms (ICFs) must now account for remote monitoring, wearable devices, and telemedicine visits. The complexity has tripled.

2. Protocols need new sections addressing digital endpoints, eConsent procedures, and home-nursing logistics.

3. CSRs must describe data collection from apps, sensors, and patient-reported outcomes captured outside traditional sites.

4. Regulatory agencies (FDA, EMA) are still catching up — meaning writers must navigate guidance documents that are evolving in real-time.

5. Data integrity narratives become critical when data flows from a patient's home rather than a controlled clinical site.

I've seen protocols where 60% of assessments happen remotely. Writing these documents requires understanding technology, patient experience, AND regulatory expectations simultaneously.

The medical writers who thrive in 2026 and beyond will be those who understand digital health infrastructure — not just ICH-GCP guidelines.

My advice: Start learning about eCOA, eConsent platforms, and real-world data integration now.
