India's biosimilar market is projected to hit $12 billion by 2030. It was $4.1 billion in 2023.

That's a near 3x growth in 7 years. And most people in pharma aren't paying enough attention.

Here's why India is positioned to dominate the global biosimilar space:

The cost advantage is staggering:
→ Developing a biosimilar in India costs $10-30 million
→ In the US/EU? $100-250 million
→ Indian manufacturers operate at 40-60% lower cost than Western counterparts

Who's leading the charge?

Biocon Biologics — Already has 5 biosimilars commercialized globally (Trastuzumab, Bevacizumab, Insulin Glargine, Adalimumab, Pegfilgrastim). Merged with Viatris biosimilars to become a top-5 global player.

Dr. Reddy's — Rituximab and Bevacizumab biosimilars in 40+ markets.

Zydus Life Sciences — Aggressive pipeline with Adalimumab and Denosumab biosimilars.

Intas/Accord — Quietly building a massive European presence.

The global context makes this explosive:
• $100B+ worth of biologics losing patent protection by 2030
• US biosimilar penetration still under 15% (Europe is at 80%+)
• Humira biosimilars alone saved the US healthcare system $6 billion in year one

For India, this isn't just about export revenue. It's about patient access. Cancer biologics that cost ₹1-3 lakh per cycle as innovators are available at ₹20,000-50,000 as biosimilars.

The documentation challenge? Biosimilar regulatory packages are MORE complex than generics — analytical similarity, comparative PK/PD, and clinical equivalence all need rigorous scientific writing.

This is India's moment to move from generic small molecules to complex biologics. The companies investing now will define the next era.

Is India ready to become the biosimilar capital of the world?
