The USFDA rejected 40% more drug applications in 2025 compared to 2023. And the #1 reason isn't bad science.

It's bad documentation.

Complete Response Letters (CRLs) are at an all-time high. And after reviewing dozens of regulatory submissions over 15+ years, I can tell you — the same documentation mistakes keep appearing.

Here are the top deficiencies cited in FDA CRLs this year:

1. Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Controls (CMC) — 38% of rejections
→ Incomplete stability data
→ Inadequate specification justification
→ Manufacturing process validation gaps
→ This isn't science failure — it's WRITING failure. The data exists but isn't presented correctly.

2. Clinical data presentation — 27% of rejections
→ Inconsistencies between CSR tables and integrated summaries
→ Missing subgroup analyses that were pre-specified in the SAP
→ Safety narratives that don't address signal patterns

3. Labeling deficiencies — 18% of rejections
→ USPI language not aligned with clinical evidence
→ Missing or inadequate risk communication
→ Dosing instructions that create ambiguity

4. Administrative/formatting — 12% of rejections
→ eCTD module cross-reference errors
→ Non-compliant PDF bookmarks
→ Broken hyperlinks in the submission

The brutal reality: companies spend $500M-$2B getting to an NDA submission, then lose 12-18 months because of documentation issues that a skilled regulatory writing team could have caught.

The cost of one CRL:
→ 12-18 months delay
→ $500M-$1.5B in lost revenue
→ Stock price impact (often 10-30% drop on CRL announcement)
→ Team morale devastation

Prevention is absurdly cheaper than cure. Invest in your regulatory writing team BEFORE the submission, not after the CRL.
