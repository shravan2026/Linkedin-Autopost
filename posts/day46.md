India exported $27.9 billion in pharma products last year — but our clinical research contribution is embarrassingly low.

Let that satisfying export number sink in. Now here's the uncomfortable truth:

India contributes less than 3% of global clinical research output despite being the "pharmacy of the world."

We manufacture 20% of the world's generic medicines. We supply 60% of global vaccine demand. Yet when it comes to designing clinical trials, generating novel data, and producing original research — we're practically invisible.

Here's what the numbers reveal:

→ India's share of global clinical trials registered on ClinicalTrials.gov: ~4.2%
→ US contribution: 42%. EU: 28%. China: 9.6%
→ Indian pharma R&D spend as % of revenue: 6-8% (vs 15-20% for global innovators)
→ Number of NCEs (New Chemical Entities) from India in last decade: Less than 5

Sun Pharma, Dr. Reddy's, Cipla — these are manufacturing giants. But they're not research powerhouses. Not yet.

The gap is real. We export tablets and APIs worth billions, but we import the science behind them.

Why does this matter for professionals like us?

Because the next wave of pharma growth won't come from generic manufacturing alone. Biosimilars, cell and gene therapies, ADCs — these require deep clinical research capabilities and world-class medical writing talent.

The opportunity is massive. India has 800+ medical colleges producing doctors every year. We have a patient population unmatched for diversity. What we lack is the infrastructure, investment, and mindset to become a clinical research superpower.

After 15 years in this industry, I believe India's pharma story will remain incomplete until we close this research gap.
