I've reviewed 500+ clinical documents in my career — here's the system I built for error-free medical writing.

After 15 years at companies like Bristol Myers Squibb, you develop a sixth sense for inconsistencies. But intuition isn't scalable. Systems are.

Here's my 7-step review framework that catches 99% of errors:

STEP 1: THE STRUCTURAL CHECK
→ Does the document follow the correct template version?
→ Are all required sections present and properly numbered?
→ Do headers match the table of contents exactly?

STEP 2: THE DATA CONSISTENCY SWEEP
→ Cross-reference every number against the statistical output
→ Check: tables vs text vs figures vs in-text citations
→ Rule: If a number appears more than once, verify it matches everywhere

STEP 3: THE TERMINOLOGY AUDIT
→ Is the drug name consistent (INN vs brand vs code)?
→ Are endpoints described identically across protocol, SAP, and CSR?
→ Check abbreviation list against first-use definitions

STEP 4: THE REGULATORY COMPLIANCE CHECK
→ Does the document meet ICH E3/E6/E9 requirements?
→ Are all agency-specific requirements addressed?
→ Is the document version-controlled correctly?

STEP 5: THE LOGIC FLOW REVIEW
→ Do conclusions follow from the results presented?
→ Is the benefit-risk narrative coherent?
→ Are limitations acknowledged appropriately?

STEP 6: THE CROSS-DOCUMENT ALIGNMENT
→ Protocol → SAP → CSR: Is the story consistent?
→ IB ↔ Protocol: Do safety sections align?

STEP 7: THE FINAL FRESH-EYES PASS
→ Read the executive summary as if you've never seen the document
→ Would a reviewer at FDA/EMA understand this without context?

This system took me years to refine. I wish someone had shared it with me in year one.
