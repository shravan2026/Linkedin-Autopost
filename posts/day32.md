$50 billion worth of branded drugs are losing patent protection between 2024 and 2028.

That's not a cliff. That's a freefall.

Here's which Big Pharma companies are staring down the barrel:

AbbVie — Humira ($21B peak sales) already lost exclusivity. Skyrizi and Rinvoq need to fill a massive hole.

Bristol Myers Squibb — Eliquis ($12.2B) and Opdivo ($9.0B) both face patent expiry by 2028. That's over 60% of revenue at risk.

Merck — Keytruda ($25B+), the world's best-selling drug, loses US exclusivity in 2028. They're acquiring everything in sight.

Pfizer — Post-COVID revenue collapse + Ibrance patent loss = existential pressure.

Johnson & Johnson — Stelara ($10.9B) already exposed to biosimilar competition.

What happens when this much revenue evaporates?

→ M&A frenzy (we're already seeing it — $200B+ in deals since 2023)
→ Aggressive lifecycle management strategies
→ Massive opportunity for generics and biosimilar companies
→ Documentation workload EXPLODES (new filings, patent challenges, regulatory submissions)

For generic companies in India, this is Christmas morning. Expect Sun Pharma, Dr. Reddy's, Cipla, and Aurobindo to file aggressively in the US.

For medical writers? This patent cliff means more NDAs, ANDAs, sBLAs, and CTD modules than we've seen in a decade. Regulatory writing teams are already stretched thin.

The companies that survive this cliff won't be those with the best drugs today — they'll be those with the best pipeline strategy and fastest regulatory execution.
