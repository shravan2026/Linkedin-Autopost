## A Day in the Life of a Scientific Writer at Big Pharma

People often ask me what a typical day looks like as a Scientific Writer II at a large pharmaceutical company. The honest answer: no two days are the same, and that is what keeps this role intellectually stimulating.

A morning might start with a cross-functional meeting — aligning with biostatisticians on data outputs for a Clinical Study Report, or discussing protocol amendments with the clinical team. By mid-morning, I am deep in document drafting, synthesizing efficacy and safety data into narratives that meet ICH-E3 guidelines.

After lunch, there might be a quality review of a colleague's Investigator's Brochure, followed by responding to regulatory queries on a submission package. Late afternoon could involve mentoring a junior writer on structuring a clinical summary or reviewing style guide updates.

What makes big pharma distinct from CRO work? The depth of involvement. You are embedded in the program from early development through submission. You see the full arc of a molecule's journey. The complexity is higher, the timelines are tighter, and the regulatory scrutiny is intense.

But the reward is equally significant — knowing that your document is part of a submission that could bring a life-changing therapy to patients.

This role demands precision, scientific acumen, and the ability to manage multiple deliverables simultaneously. It is challenging, and it is exactly where I want to be.
