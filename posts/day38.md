60% of clinical trials fail. The average cost of bringing a drug to market? $2.6 billion.

Do the math. That's over $1.5 TRILLION wasted globally on failed drug development over the past decade.

And here's the uncomfortable truth nobody in pharma leadership wants to hear: a significant chunk of these failures were preventable. Not with better molecules. With better protocol writing.

Where protocol design failures kill trials:

1. Overly complex eligibility criteria
→ 86% of trials fail to recruit on time
→ The average protocol has 31 eligibility criteria (up from 10 in the 1990s)
→ Each additional criterion reduces the eligible population by 15-20%

2. Wrong primary endpoints
→ 25% of Phase III failures are due to endpoint selection, not drug efficacy
→ Regulatory agencies reject endpoints that don't align with guidance — this is a WRITING and strategy problem

3. Protocol amendments
→ Average trial undergoes 2.3 substantial amendments
→ Each amendment costs $450,000-$535,000 and delays timelines by 3+ months
→ Most are avoidable with better upfront protocol development

4. Unrealistic visit schedules
→ Patient burden drives 30% of dropouts
→ Retention failure = underpowered studies = inconclusive results

What better protocol writing looks like:
✓ Patient-centric design from Day 1
✓ Feasibility-tested endpoints before finalization
✓ Decentralized trial elements reducing site burden
✓ Plain language protocols for site staff and patients
