ICH E6(R2) GCP and Its Impact on Medical Writing

Good Clinical Practice isn't just for clinical operations teams. As medical writers, GCP principles directly shape how we approach every document we create.

ICH E6(R2) introduced a risk-based approach to clinical trials, and this has rippled through our work in meaningful ways:

📋 Protocol Design – E6(R2) emphasizes proportionate monitoring. Protocols now need clearer risk assessments, and medical writers must articulate these monitoring strategies precisely.

📋 Informed Consent Forms – The guideline reinforces truly informed consent. ICFs must be written in language participants genuinely understand — not just legally compliant boilerplate.

📋 Data Integrity – The emphasis on certified copies and audit trails means our source documentation references must be airtight.

📋 Quality Management Systems – We now document quality tolerance limits in protocols. Medical writers need to understand what these limits mean and how they're operationalized.

📋 Essential Documents – E6(R2) expanded the essential documents list. Writers involved in TMF management must understand what constitutes a complete, inspection-ready file.

Why this matters for your career:

Medical writers who understand GCP deeply are better collaborators. When you grasp why a clinical operations team requests specific protocol language, you produce better documents faster.

I make it a practice to revisit ICH E6 annually. The guidelines evolve, and so should our understanding.

GCP isn't overhead — it's the foundation of credible clinical research.
