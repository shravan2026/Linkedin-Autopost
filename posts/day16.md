How to Handle Deficiency Letters from Regulatory Agencies

Receiving a deficiency letter (or List of Questions from EMA) can feel daunting. But in 15+ years of regulatory writing, I've learned that these are opportunities — not setbacks.

Here's my approach to handling them effectively:

🔹 **Don't panic — analyze** — Read every question carefully. Categorize them: data clarification, additional analysis needed, or strategic positioning required. Not all questions carry equal weight.

🔹 **Assemble the right team immediately** — Deficiency responses are cross-functional efforts. Medical writers, biostatisticians, clinical scientists, and regulatory strategists all need to be at the table from day one.

🔹 **Answer what's asked** — This sounds obvious, but I've seen responses that dance around questions or provide excessive background. Be direct. Address the specific concern, provide the supporting data, and move on.

🔹 **Use the Q&A format strategically** — Restate the question, provide a concise response, and reference supporting data (tables, figures, appendices). Make the reviewer's job easy.

🔹 **Maintain consistency** — Ensure your responses align with what's already in the submission dossier. Contradictions will generate more questions.

🔹 **Timeline discipline** — Response windows are tight. Build in time for internal review, legal review, and QC before submission.

A well-crafted deficiency response can turn regulatory skepticism into confidence. It's high-stakes writing — and one of the most impactful things a medical writer can do.
