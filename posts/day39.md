A senior medical writer in the US earns $160,000-$210,000 per year. In India? ₹18-35 lakhs ($21,000-$42,000).

Same ICH guidelines. Same therapeutic areas. Same quality expectations. 4-8x salary difference.

Let's talk about the elephant in the room — pharma salaries for medical writers across both markets. With actual numbers.

INDIA (2026 data):
→ Entry-level (0-3 years): ₹5-12 LPA
→ Mid-level (3-7 years): ₹12-22 LPA
→ Senior (7-12 years): ₹22-35 LPA
→ Principal/Lead (12+ years): ₹35-55 LPA
→ Director level: ₹55-80 LPA (rare, mostly MNCs)

UNITED STATES (2026 data):
→ Entry-level: $85,000-$110,000
→ Mid-level: $110,000-$145,000
→ Senior: $145,000-$185,000
→ Principal/Director: $185,000-$250,000+
→ Freelance rates: $85-$150/hour

THE GAP IS CLOSING (slowly):
→ Indian medical writer salaries grew 12-15% annually post-COVID
→ Remote work for US/EU clients pays ₹50-80 LPA for experienced writers
→ Freelancers working with global CROs earn $40-$70/hour from India

Why the gap still exists:
• Cost of living adjustment (partially valid)
• Market perception that Indian writing = "cheaper quality" (mostly invalid)
• Lack of salary transparency in Indian pharma
• Oversupply at entry level in India

What's changing:
• Quality-conscious sponsors now pay premium for experienced Indian writers
• Biocon, Novartis India, BMS India, and IQVIA India offering competitive packages
• The "location arbitrage" era is ending as companies realize talent is global

My honest take: Indian medical writers are undervalued by at least 30-40% even after adjusting for cost of living.
